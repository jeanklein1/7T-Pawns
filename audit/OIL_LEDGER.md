### The verification pass (V1, V2)

Nothing on this branch was compiled — no toolchain or Dawn headers exist in the audit container — so the batch was put through an adversarial read instead: fifteen skeptics, one per unit plus a whole-branch compile-surface sweep, each instructed to hunt compile breakage first and behavioral delta second, and to verify commit-message claims against the code rather than trust them.

**Every unit came back CLEAN on both axes.** The sweep raised one BEHAVIORAL_DELTA against U9, which I then **refuted in its specifics**: it argued a finite world drains allocations over ~7 budgeted frames while the pawn walks out of the scan box, but the `fullRegen` bootstrap allocates the whole finite window *unbudgeted* on the world's first frame and nothing evicts in finite mode, so there is no drain to outrun. The structural half of the observation survived, though — the candidate set is `box(pawn cell) ∩ window(last_center)`, the box follows the pawn, and no raiser tracked it; in finite mode the window is pinned while the box is not. That unreachability rested on a conjunction of three facts in three files, so `V1` gave the box its own two-int raiser and the gate stopped depending on why box and window agree.

`V1` also took the ROSTER gate onto the hoisted gallery binds (a gallery-less build was paying two binds per pass for zero draws — a cost the ROSTER doctrine forbids a disabled piece) and removed a dead group0 head bind in the main pass. `V2` wrote down the invariants the batch *created*: the BeatClock write window, the ribbon pose-width pairing invariant, the two surviving derivations of the agent weight sums, and the two raises `evict_patch` owes but leaves to its caller.

Two reading artifacts of the meter itself were found and left alone, both instrument-build-only: the census frame's own blocking print lands in the fresh window as `S frame_total max`, and the first window's S means divide N−1 samples by N (every later window is exact).

### U14 — STOPPED: the pinned wrapper is not inspectable from here

The unit's precondition was to verify a **non-allocating MapAsync path in the pinned Dawn wrapper**. Neither wrapper version is knowable from this container: the native twin points `DAWN_DIR` at a local path (`C:/dev/dawn`) with no tag or commit, and the web twin takes `--use-port=emdawnwebgpu`, whose version follows the installed emsdk. Writing against an unverified signature would either fail to compile for both twins or bind a different overload with different lifetime semantics — so the unit holds, per the handoff's own condition.

**What the recon did establish** (upstream `google/dawn@main`, `generator/templates/api_cpp.h` — the template both wrappers are generated from): the callback methods expose **two** overloads. The single-argument form is the allocating one (`Create(F lambda)` → `std::make_unique<F>` when `CppFTraits<F>::capturing`), and this is the one both readbacks and the meter use today. A **non-allocating** form exists — `Create(F lambda, T userdata)`, constrained `requires (!CppFTraits<F>::capturing)` — taking a non-capturing callable plus explicit userdata.

**The design the conversion wants, if the pin carries that overload.** The callbacks capture two things (`this` and `gen`), but the typed overload carries one userdata. `this` becomes the userdata; the issued world-gen moves to a member beside its state machine (e.g. `pawnReadbackGen_`), written when the copy is armed and read in the callback. That is exact, not approximate: the skip-if-busy law already guarantees at most one readback per machine in flight, so a single member per machine cannot alias, and the gen is still captured at issue time — the P5 guard's meaning is unchanged.

**To land it, Jean:** grep the pinned `webgpu_cpp.h` for `CppFTraits` and the two-argument `CallbackInfoHelper::Create`. If both are present in both twins' headers, the conversion is mechanical and OIL_2 can take it.

### U15 — STOPPED: the census is complete, and that is what stops it

The pre-check was to skip the three `collect_sorted_patches` walks plus the budget walk on frames with no pending work. The transition census was carried out in full, and it does not close inside this module:

* **Eviction scan** (`!in_render_window`) goes non-empty on: a `last_center` change (gridChanged); a shrink of `active_radius`; and a budget-capped eviction pass. The radius has a writer **in `direction/input.hpp`** — a keypress door that resizes the active window with no gridChanged. (The finite-mode cap inside `stream_patches` is re-applied every frame and rides `reset_surface`, so transitions are covered.)
* **Spawn scan** (`phase == ALLOCATED`) goes non-empty on: either allocation site, and a capped spawn pass.
* **Generation scan** (`phase == SPAWNED || NEEDS_REGEN`) goes non-empty on: `spawn_selected_patches` (→SPAWNED), `mark_patches_for_regen` **in `machine/entity_pipeline.hpp`** (→NEEDS_REGEN, the pyramid-bake path), and a capped generation pass.

So the guarantee would rest on a hand-maintained invariant spanning three modules, two of them outside the surface machine, with no structural enforcement — and the handoff makes this guarantee authority-bearing: a missed raiser is a stalled stream, a visible defect. The flag form is exactly the shape that breaks silently when a future edit adds a fourth raiser.

**The form that would be safe** is a registry epoch bumped by the phase writes themselves (a setter, so a new phase write cannot forget to bump it), with the scans gated on `epoch != last_scanned_epoch`. That is a new mechanism, and house law is no mechanism until a measurement asks — the same law that defers D1. The meter run answers it: R3's residual CPU row is three ≤225-entry integer walks with no allocation and no GPU traffic, so if `stream_patches` reads as noise beside the budget, the mechanism is not wanted; if it reads heavy, OIL_2 has the census above and the epoch design ready.
